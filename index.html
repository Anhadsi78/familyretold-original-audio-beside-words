Keep the original recording beside the words you review

When someone tells a family story, two things are worth keeping: the recording itself, and a reviewed text that relatives can read. Those are not the same file. The recording holds the voice. The text holds names, a searchable copy, and a version that is easier to follow if the audio is hard to hear.

The original stays as it was

FamilyRetold keeps them apart on purpose. The original recording stays as it was, beside the reviewed words, and is not replaced by them. On the web, you record in a supported browser or type the story if talking feels like too much. Browser transcription depends on the browser, and you can type or review the words yourself. There is no external AI rewriting or ghostwriting of the story.

Label copies, and keep your own backup

If you later make a shorter or quieter listening copy to share, save the untouched original separately and label which is which. Do not treat a transcript as permission to invent a missed word; if something is unclear, mark the uncertainty.

Photographs and short keepsake notes can sit with the same story. When the stories add up, the archive can show a timeline, a book preview, illustrated pages to print at home, a downloadable PDF, and a portable JSON backup. FamilyRetold does not print, bind or ship books, and the service is not a permanent storage guarantee, so keep independent copies of irreplaceable recordings.
